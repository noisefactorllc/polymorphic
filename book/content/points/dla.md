Particles wander at random until they bump into something, then stick there
permanently and become part of what the next particle can bump into. From a
single seed, coral grows.

Why it branches instead of filling in solidly is the interesting part. A
wandering particle is far more likely to run into a tip sticking out into open
space than to find its way down into a crevice between two existing branches,
because the branches get in the way first. So every protrusion grows a little
faster than every hollow, and that small advantage compounds relentlessly.
Nothing tells it to make branches. Branches are what shadowing does to random
walkers.

This process is called diffusion-limited aggregation, and it shows up in
electrodeposits, mineral dendrites, lightning and frost on a window, for exactly
the same reason.
