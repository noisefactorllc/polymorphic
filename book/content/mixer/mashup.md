Up to eight pictures routed into one, steered by a ninth in greyscale.

The control picture's brightness is divided into bands, and each band shows a
different source. Darkest areas show the first, next darkest show the second,
and so on up to white.

The bands do not meet at hard lines. Each boundary cross-fades into the next, so
the handover between sources follows the control picture's own gradients. That
is the difference between this and a posterised stencil with pictures pasted
into the holes: where the control image is smooth, the sources dissolve into
each other smoothly.

Any input left unconnected falls back to showing the control picture itself, so
a half-wired mashup still renders something you can look at and reason about
rather than dropping to black.
