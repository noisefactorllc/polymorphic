A quilt. The picture becomes a grid of solid squares, each one raised slightly
and stitched to its neighbours.

Every square takes a single colour from a small average around its middle, and
takes its height from how bright that colour is. Bright squares stand tall, dark
ones sit low.

The bevel around each square is what makes it read as needlework rather than
tile. Every rim is compared against the square next to it. A rim beside a lower
neighbour is exposed and catches the light; a rim beside a taller one is in its
shade. All of it is lit from one consistent direction.

Because height came from brightness in the first place, the lighting agrees with
the tone that produced it: light squares are the ones that stand up and catch
the light. That agreement is why the surface looks sculpted rather than
speckled.
