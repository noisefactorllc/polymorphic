Three related looks that all treat the picture as a carved surface lit from an
angle: Bas Relief, Plaster, and Note Paper.

The picture is blurred, brightness is read as height, and the slope at each
point decides whether it catches the light or falls into shadow.

The modes differ in what they do to the height before lighting it. Bas Relief
lights it as it is, giving a shallow carving. Plaster flattens it into plateaus
with steep sides first and inverts it, so dark areas rise, then makes the shading
glossy, which is what gives it the look of moulded plaster. Note Paper cuts the
height into a hard two-tone shape, adds a beveled band along the boundary, and
sprinkles grain across the result, so it reads as torn and pressed paper.

`smoothness` sets the blur, and so how coarse the shapes the carving follows are.
