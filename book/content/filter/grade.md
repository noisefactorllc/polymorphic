A full colour grading suite, the sort of chain a colourist works through on a
film. Twenty-two presets set it all at once if you want a look rather than
control.

The stages run in the order a professional would use them: exposure and white
balance first, then contrast and the tone curve, then the three-way colour
wheels that tint shadows, midtones and highlights separately, then saturation,
then split toning.

Two are worth singling out. `vibrance` is not the same as saturation: it scales
each pixel by how unsaturated it already is, so muted colours come up while
already-vivid ones are left alone. It is the one to reach for on skin.

The HSL secondary at the end is a keyer. It isolates a range of hue, saturation
and brightness with a soft edge and corrects only inside it, which is how you
change a sky without touching anyone standing under it. All of it runs in
linear light.
