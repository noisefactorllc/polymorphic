A single stray hair across the picture, of the kind that gets caught on a
scanner glass or in a projector gate.

It uses the same agents as `fibers`, tuned for scarcity instead of texture. The
population drops to a fraction of a percent, so only a handful exist, and each
one is given a long life and a strong tendency to turn, which means it wanders a
great distance and curls as it goes. The result is a few individual strands
rather than a mat of them.

Colour does the rest. Where `fibers` gives every thread a bright random hue,
these are near-black and slightly transparent, at a line width that scales with
the output size. That is enough to read as a real hair sitting on top of the
image rather than as something drawn into it. `density` adds more if one is not
irritating enough.
