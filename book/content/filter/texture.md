Surface texture: canvas weave, watercolour paper, stucco, brushed metal and
eleven more. The picture is not distorted at all, only lit, as though it had
been printed onto that surface and a light were raking across it.

A height pattern is generated, the slope is measured by comparing four
neighbouring points, and that slope decides which parts catch the light and
which fall into shadow.

The fifteen patterns are built quite differently from one another. Canvas
multiplies two waves crossing at right angles to weave a grid. Crosshatch does
the same on the diagonal. Paper stacks a few rounds of creased noise. The
material surfaces use noise with deliberately uneven cell shapes, stretched long
in one direction, so fibres emerge from the structure of the pattern rather than
from smearing it afterwards.
