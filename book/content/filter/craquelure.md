The fine web of cracks that opens up in old varnish as a painting ages.

A cell pattern lays out where the cracks run. The clever part is the lighting.
The crack pattern is treated as a surface with height, but inverted, so a crack
is a groove cut into the picture rather than a thread laid on top of it. That
one sign flip is the whole illusion: it decides which wall of the groove catches
the light and which drops into shadow. Get it backwards and the cracks look like
raised string.

The surface is lit from the upper left, and the result is multiplied into the
picture so the colours underneath show through. Flat areas nowhere near a crack
are held at exactly untouched rather than very slightly tinted, which keeps the
effect confined to the cracks instead of casting a faint haze over everything.
