A rubber stamp impression. Two flat colours, ink and paper, and nothing in
between.

The picture is blurred, its brightness is cut at a threshold, and the two sides
become the two colours. The blur is doing the real work here. Cut a sharp
picture at a threshold and the boundary follows every fleck of texture, giving a
ragged noisy mess. Blur it first and the boundary follows only the big shapes,
ignoring detail entirely, which is exactly what a stamp does when it reduces a
picture to a single cut shape.

So `smoothness` is really a control over how coarse the shapes are that survive
the cut, not how soft the edges look.

A separate roughness control wobbles the threshold slightly, giving the edge the
torn, uneven quality of ink pressed off real rubber rather than a
mathematically perfect line.
