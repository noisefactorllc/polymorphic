The old cinematographer's trick of smearing petroleum jelly around the edge of a
lens, leaving the centre sharp and the borders soft and glowing. It was how you
flattered an ageing star.

The blur samples the picture at 32 points arranged in a spiral rather than on a
grid, which spreads them evenly over a disc and avoids the sampling pattern
showing through. The result is brightened slightly and mixed back in, so the
soft areas glow rather than merely going soft.

What confines it to the edges is a mask measuring distance from the centre, but
measured squarely rather than radially, so its contours are rectangles following
the frame rather than circles. The middle of the picture stays completely
untouched and the effect ramps in toward the border. `alpha` sets the strength.
