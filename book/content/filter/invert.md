A photographic negative. Every value flips: black becomes white, white becomes
black, and each colour swaps for its opposite.

`mode` offers a second, stranger option called solarize. Instead of flipping
everything, it leaves the dark half of the range alone and folds the bright half
back down. The effect is that both pure black and pure white come out black,
while the midtones stay bright. Tones that were far apart end up next to each
other, which is why solarized images look metallic and unreal.

This is not an invention of image editors. Overexpose a piece of film part way
through developing it and the highlights reverse in exactly this way, a mishap
photographers noticed and then started doing on purpose. It carries the name of
Armand Sabattier, who wrote it up in 1862.
