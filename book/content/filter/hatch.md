Five hand-drawn looks, all built from the same idea: a field of noise stretched
out along one direction until it stops looking like noise and starts looking
like pencil strokes.

Where the strokes land is decided by tone. Dark parts of the picture admit more
ink, light parts admit less, which is exactly how you shade a drawing by hand.

The modes differ in what they do with that field. Graphic Pen is a single hard
cut, the starkest of the five. Charcoal adds a second, shorter layer of strokes
for roughness and keeps its ink down in the shadows. Chalk and Charcoal works on
a mid-grey ground with two layers of strokes crossing at right angles, dark
strokes filling the shadows and light ones the highlights. Conte Crayon fills
the midtones with texture instead of a smooth gradient.

`pressure` means something a little different in each: how much coverage, how
dark, or how sharply the tone gate cuts.
