An old television picture, complete with scanlines and the bulge of curved
glass.

The trick is that the picture and the scanlines live on different surfaces. The
glass curvature is worked out first from a slowly shifting noise field, and then
the scanlines are drawn onto that curved surface while the image itself stays
flat behind it. Because the two are on separate planes, the bars slide across
the picture as the warp moves, exactly as a reflection slides across a real
screen when you shift in your seat. Warping both together would just look like a
bent picture and nothing more.

The scanlines are combined in a way that both adds and multiplies, so bright
bars get a lift while dark ones are crushed further down. That asymmetry is what
gives a tube its glow instead of a flat grey striping.
