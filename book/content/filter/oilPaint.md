Six painterly treatments, all built on one idea worth understanding.

Look at the ring of pixels around any point and split it into eight wedges. Some
of those wedges will be busy, with a lot of variation, and some will be calm.
Take the average colour of the calmest one and use that. Do it everywhere and
something useful happens: flat areas smooth out, but edges stay razor sharp,
because a wedge that straddles an edge is never the calm one. It never averages
across a boundary, only along it, which is why the result reads as brush strokes
laid down with intent rather than as a blur.

That is the Kuwahara filter, and it is one of the few ways to soften an image
without softening its edges.
